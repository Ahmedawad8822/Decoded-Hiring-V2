# Hiring Signal Decay and Decision Quality

When hiring, every signal has a shelf life. The longer you wait between
completing a candidate interview loop and making a hiring decision, the more
that signal degrades — not because the candidate changes, but because your own
memory of them fades and your comparison set grows noisier.

## The 48-Hour Window

Research across hiring teams consistently shows that interview signal quality
drops measurably after 48 hours. After this window, raters begin to conflate
recent candidates with earlier ones, and recall bias begins to distort scores.

## Evidence-Based Hiring Principles

Evidence-based hiring means grounding every hiring decision in structured
observation, not gut feeling. The key principle is that every signal must be
tied to a specific job requirement and scored against a calibrated rubric
before the debrief meeting takes place.

## Time-Bounded Decision Rituals

The fix for signal decay is a time-boxed decision ritual. After the last
interview, the hiring team has exactly 24 hours to submit scores, discuss,
and reach a decision. No exceptions, no "let me talk to my manager tomorrow."
The window is enforced by a calendar deadline that the system owns.

## Anchoring on the Last Interview

Teams that schedule final interviews early in the week tend to see higher
hiring consistency. This happens because the last interview is still fresh in
memory when the debrief happens, and the natural tendency is to anchor the
final candidate assessment on that most recent data point.

## Calibration Over Aggregation

Simply averaging scores across interviewers is not enough. The real signal
comes from calibration — where the team has explicitly discussed and
reconciled differences in scoring standards before the final decision is made.
